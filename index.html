01 / Profile

A legal career built on curiosity, precision, and people.

I like the point where a rule stops being abstract and starts affecting a real person, family, business, or community.

I came to law through a mix of interests: how institutions make decisions, how those decisions affect people, and how the facts of a case can change the way a rule operates in real life.

Family law and child welfare are areas I have studied deeply, but I do not see my career as limited to one practice area. I’m equally interested in the day-to-day craft of legal practice, from research and drafting to litigation support, trusts and estates, and investigative work.

My experience with the New Jersey Office of the Attorney General reinforced what I enjoy most: reading closely, organizing complicated records, asking the next question, and helping turn information into something that can move a matter forward.

01
Featured research · Independent legal research · 2025

Best Interests or State Interests?

Law and Psychology in New Jersey Termination of Parental Rights

A multidisciplinary examination of New Jersey’s termination-of-parental-rights framework and the “best interests of the child” standard. The paper asks whether permanency timelines and administrative interests can overshadow children’s developmental needs, using New Jersey case law alongside research in attachment theory, foster-care instability, and childhood trauma.

Family LawChild WelfareConstitutional RightsDevelopmental Psychology
Why I chose it

This was my final major jurisprudence research project and the work that most clearly reflects my interest in family law, child welfare, and the human consequences of legal decision-making.
